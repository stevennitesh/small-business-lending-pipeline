The question

Give the totals a useful comparison.

The U.S. Small Business Administration (SBA) publishes historical 7(a) and 504 loan approval records. Dollar totals describe the scale of that activity. Record counts describe its volume. Comparing activity with employer business locations adds geographic context. Each view answers a different question.

A lending analyst or economic-development researcher could use these comparisons to choose states, sectors or lender assignments for closer study. The results do not establish credit access, unmet demand or the effect of SBA programs.

Activity over time · 2025

More reported dollars, fewer approval records.

In 2025, reported amounts rose to $40.93 billion and approval records fell to 74,098 from 81,081 in 2024. The dollar change is 1.71%; the record change is -8.61%. A dollar trend alone misses the change in volume.

Read the average with its denominator. The $552,334 average divides reported dollars by 74,098 records with known amounts. A zero amount is known; a missing amount stays out of this denominator.

Geography & context · 2023

California leads dollars. Ohio leads relative activity.

California leads 2023 reported dollars at $5.06 billion. Ohio leads approval records per 1,000 employer business locations at 15.92. The two rankings answer different questions about the same year.

Why use 2023 here? It is the complete context year used by these inputs. Census employer establishments are business locations with employees, across firm sizes. They exclude businesses without employees and do not count eligible small-business borrowers.

Census measures an annual March 12 stock; SBA activity spans the calendar year. Records per 1,000 locations are a contextual comparison, with different source scopes and observation windows. They do not measure lending penetration.

Programs & industries · 2025

SBA 7(a) accounts for 82.33% of reported amounts.

SBA 7(a) accounts for 82.33% of reported 2025 amounts. SBA 504 accounts for 17.67% of reported 2025 amounts. SBA 7(a) supports general business uses such as working capital, equipment and real estate. SBA 504 supports major fixed assets.

The programs report different amount bases. 7(a) reports whole loans. 504 reports the SBA/Certified Development Company portion. The combined total does not measure total project financing or comparable government exposure.

Why some annual labor comparisons are unavailable

October 2025 unemployment data was not published. The available 11-month mean is descriptive; the model suppresses an ordinary comparable annual change for that year. A missing publisher release is different from a failed download.

What I built

Preserve the meaning from source file to report.

My contribution covers source adapters and validation, Python/Prefect orchestration, dbt models and tests, reporting contracts, reproducible charts and a seven-page local Power BI report. Public agencies supply the data. DuckDB, dbt, Prefect and Power BI supply the processing tools.

Define the population once

dbt owns record eligibility, period alignment and additive components. Reporting sums dollars and known-amount counts before division, so a state-level average cannot distort the combined result.

Test plausible wrong answers

Small SQL fixtures use unequal groups, missing values, absent periods and changing state sets. They distinguish the intended formulas from results that look reasonable but use the wrong denominator.

Measure the whole refresh

A broad intermediate table saved about four seconds in one model, but added a 14–15 second build and hundreds of megabytes. Full refreshes became slower, so I rejected it.

Read the recorded performance result and its limits

A May 2026 native DuckDB CSV-load comparison recorded wall time of about 28.6 → 18.1 seconds and maximum resident memory of about 4.7 → 2.0 GB on the same retained inputs. Source-table row counts matched. This is a historical observation on an earlier population.

The wrapper timed one command invocation and recorded child-process maximum resident memory; it did not measure combined process-tree RAM. The retained summary lacks hardware, environment versions, cache conditions and repeat counts. It cannot establish a controlled speedup or performance for the current larger warehouse. What the findings support

A descriptive analysis with inspectable evidence.

  • Activity, concentration and context. These comparisons support a closer look at changes over time and differences across states, sectors and assigned lender names.
  • A defined slice of public lending data. SBA approvals include canceled or not-funded records. They do not represent all small-business credit, borrower funding, unique borrowers or inflation-adjusted activity.
  • An engineering portfolio. The work demonstrates data validation, SQL metric design and reproducible reporting. It does not claim business deployment, causal impact, credit-access measurement or borrower risk prediction.
Definitions & methods Populations, formulas and comparison windows

Use the denominator that answers the question.

Reported approval amounts
Sum of valid, nonnegative nominal source amounts. Whole-loan amounts for 7(a), SBA/Certified Development Company (CDC) portions for 504. Missing amounts do not add dollars.
Approval records
Count of eligible source records with a recognized project state and calendar approval date. Cancellation/not-funded statuses stay in scope.
Average reported amount
Summed dollars ÷ summed records with known amounts. Zero is known. Missing amounts remain in record volume but leave this denominator.
Annual dollar change
(Current full-year dollars ÷ prior full-year dollars) − 1. Requires consecutive comparable years and matching observed state sets.
Approval records per 1,000 employer locations
1,000 × matched state-year records ÷ matched employer establishments. Both components use the same matched rows. Annual stocks provide context across firm sizes, rather than an eligible-borrower count.
Program share
Program dollars ÷ all-program dollars in the selected state/year population.
Known-industry share and coverage
Sector dollars ÷ all known-sector dollars. Coverage is known-sector dollars ÷ all dollars. Missing/unclassified sectors are disclosed separately.
Top-five lender-name share and coverage
Sum of the five largest known-name dollar totals ÷ all known-name dollars, after selection-wide aggregation. Coverage is known-name dollars ÷ all dollars. Ties use a stable name key.

SBA calendar years, Census March 12 establishment stocks and BLS monthly state rates have different observation windows. A matching year gives regional context without certifying identical temporal exposure. State unemployment rates are not averaged into a national rate.
